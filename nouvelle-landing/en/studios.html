Our approach

Every exchange about music or SFX costs time: you describe, propose, implement, test, start over. With LayerPitch, the studio and the composer or sound designer work in the same space, with tools designed to validate earlier, and so spend less. The music is already interactive, versioning has never been easier, and SFX get their own section.

What changes
  • A shared MoodboardGather references, images, videos, notes and tracks, and build the sound direction with your composer or sound designer before production. A clear line from the start means fewer revisions later.
  • ShowcasesPresent your game's music on a public page for players, or in a secret-link showcase for a publisher, with your visuals and colors, and know who opened it.
  • Collaborators, in one placeInvite composers, sound designers and team members into the Project: discussion, notes on tracks and videos, centralized files. No more hunting for the latest version.
  • Test without shipping a buildVideo versioning lets you quickly try which music, triggers and SFX work on the level you just built. No more sharing a build for every test.

Hi, I'm Jules and I'm a composer.

Recently, I pitched music to a video game studio. I wanted to go all in and present adaptive music directly — but I couldn't find any technical solution that actually worked for that... and I didn't want to flatten all my work into some dumb MP3.

LayerPitch was born from that frustration. One thing led to another, and the idea took shape — to the point of now being supported by Plaine Images / Take Off Inspiration, the music-tech incubator partnered with Sacem.

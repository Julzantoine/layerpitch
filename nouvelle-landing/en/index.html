What the research says

Sound matters for immersion, and so does its fit with the action.

Studies on the gaming experience link music and sound design to stronger engagement, and suggest that music works best when it is in step with what the player is experiencing.

A meta-analysis of 14 studies concludes that sound and music boost player engagement: enjoyment, flow, immersion, motivation, with a significant effect on emotional response.

Heikkilä, Danso & Luck (2025), preprint, DOI 10.33767/osf.io/mxrgh_v3

60 players played Batman: Arkham City with music that adapts to the action, or with fixed music. Adaptive music affects the feeling of living the story; the feeling of being in the game's space depends more on the music's intensity.

Gasselseder (2014), Re-Scoring the Game's Score, IADIS Int. Journal on WWW/Internet

Playing with or without music significantly changes several dimensions of the experience, such as how "dangerous" the game feels.

Lipscomb & Zehnder (2004), Immersion in the virtual environment, J. Physiological Anthropology

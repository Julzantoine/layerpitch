The problem

Your job is to make the player's experience smoother. Not to make the studio's job harder by handing them new problems.

An MP3 tells you how your music sounds. It says nothing about how it works — the layers, the transitions, the logic behind it.

The game dev/studio has no way of knowing what it would sound like in the middle of a fight, during quiet exploration, or in a boss fight at 10% health.

A lot of game devs don't actually have a clear picture of what interactive music even is, or what it could bring to their game — as long as they can't touch it, it stays abstract.

With LayerPitch, you show them a whole music system, in a way that's simple and approachable — and you let the person on the other side picture your music in their game. Result: better odds of winning the gig.

What changes
  • 5 adaptive playback modesFrom classic vertical layering to player-driven branching.
  • Live controlThe person on the other end plays with your music, adjusting intensity, muting layers, exploring the branches themselves.
  • Just a linkNo install, no zip file to warily unpack. It opens like a web page.
  • Fully customizableEvery AdReel can be styled entirely to your image, or to the studio you're targeting.
The follow-up

Once it's sent, you're not left guessing.

Every AdReel tells you who opened it, which layers they tried, how long they stayed. No more wondering whether the studio actually played with your system — or just skimmed the page.

Recently, I pitched music to a video game studio. I wanted to go all in and present adaptive music directly — but I couldn't find any technical solution that actually worked for that... and I didn't want to flatten all my work into some dumb MP3.

LayerPitch was born from that frustration. One thing led to another, and the idea took shape — to the point of now being supported by Plaine Images / Take Off Inspiration, the music-tech incubator partnered with Sacem.
